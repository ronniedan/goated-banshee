The invitation from festival director Milo Rau to Peter Thiel is not a neutral curatorial decision, nor an exercise in “free speech”. It is a political act: providing a platform to a public figure whose politics are rooted in racism, misogyny, transphobia, anti-migrant violence, supporting war and the active targeting of vulnerable communities means participating in the legitimization of those politics. Providing such figures with an institutional platform means giving a stage to right narratives and inhuman politics. It contributes to the normalization of violence.

Even in the act of un-inviting, the festival director positions Peter Thiel as someone worth having.
The invitation was the choice that mattered. Cancelling does not undo the act of providing Peter Thiel a stage, nor the fact that Milo Rau decided Peter Thiel belonged on that stage. The fact remains: he should never have been invited in the first place.

Critical framing does not protect against this normalization. Critical framing in this context is rather symbolic, considering the power imbalance between Peter Thiel, a (not just) Billionaire and Milo Rau.
We, a part of the group of students participating in the program TRANSMYTHICAL feel forced to position ourselves, in regards to the recent events surrounding the invitation from Milo Rau to Peter Thiel within the framework of WFW.
We declare:
We reject the idea that every position deserves a stage. Not every ideology deserves amplification. Not every form of violence deserves to be reframed as debate.
We are told that controversy generates discussion. We answer that some forms of power already occupy more than enough space and should not be legitimized by a discussion of the matter. Figures like Peter Thiel backed by disproportionate wealth, institutional support, media visibility, and political influence do not need additional platforms.
The deeper question is not what such a person will say.
The challenge is resisting the temptation to turn power into spectacle.
The role of cultural institutions should not be to reproduce dominant power structures nor to create a platform that works against marginalized groups.
We as artists, students, and cultural workers, refuse to become part of a cultural and marketing apparatus that transforms oppression into media spectacle and political violence into intellectual entertainment.
Our response is not motivated by a desire to censor divergent opinions, but by a refusal to participate in the amplification of narratives that undermine the dignity, safety, and rights of others.
Our opposition is not about censorship. We are not asking for silence. But, we are refusing to be
complicit in an act of normalization of anti-democratic and authoritarian politics.
